Area of Operation IV. Preflight Lesson on a Maneuver to be Performed in Flight
Note: The evaluator asks the applicant to present a preflight lesson on the selected maneuver as the lesson would be taught to a student and determines the outcome of this Task before the flight portion of the practical test. Previously developed lesson plans from the instructor applicant's library may be used.
Task A. Maneuver Lesson
References: FAA-H-8083-2, FAA-H-8083-3, FAA-H-8083-9, FAA-H-8083-23, FAA-H-8083-25; POH/AFM
Objective: To determine the applicant understands the elements associated with a maneuver Task selected from Area of Operation VII through Area of Operation XII (ASEL, ASES) or Area of Operation VII through Area of Operation XIII (AMEL, AMES) and applies that knowledge when delivering ground instruction.

Knowledge: The applicant demonstrates instructional knowledge by describing and explaining:
K1. Purpose of the maneuver.
K2. Elements of the maneuver and the associated common errors.
K3. Desired outcome(s), including completion standards.

Risk Management: The applicant explains and teaches how to identify and manage risk associated with:
R1. The selected maneuver Task.

Skills: The applicant exhibits the skill to:
S1. Deliver instruction on the selected maneuver using a lesson plan, teaching methods, and teaching aids, as appropriate, that incorporate K1 through K3.


Purpose of the maneuver.

Why is it essential for flight instructors to explain the purpose of each maneuver to their students during flight training?
• Helps students understand the practical application of the maneuver in real-world flight situations.
• Provides context and relevance to the training, enhancing student engagement and motivation.
• Allows students to grasp the underlying principles and objectives behind each maneuver, facilitating better comprehension and retention.
• Helps students anticipate and recognize the need for specific maneuvers during flight, leading to improved situational awareness and decision-making skills.
• Enables students to correlate the maneuver with relevant FAA standards and performance criteria, ensuring they meet regulatory requirements for proficiency and safety.
• Encourages a deeper understanding of aerodynamics, aircraft control, and the overall flight environment, contributing to the development of well-rounded and competent pilots.

Elements of the maneuver and the associated common errors.

What are the key elements of a maneuver that a flight instructor should emphasize to their students?
Proper setup:
Emphasize the importance of establishing the correct configuration, airspeed, altitude, and trim before initiating the maneuver.
Smooth and coordinated control inputs:
Stress the need for precise control inputs to achieve the desired outcome without over-controlling or inducing instability.
Visual references:
Teach students to use external visual cues, instruments, and references to maintain orientation and alignment throughout the maneuver.
Timing and sequence:
Guide students on the correct sequence of actions and timing required to execute the maneuver safely and effectively.
Scan and awareness:
Encourage students to maintain a vigilant scan of flight instruments, outside references, and traffic to ensure situational awareness.

How can instructors address common errors associated with these elements?
Improper setup:
Address errors related to incorrect configuration or inadequate preparation by reinforcing the importance of thorough pre-maneuver checks and establishing proper parameters.
Over-controlling:
Correct excessive or abrupt control inputs by encouraging students to develop a gentle and precise touch, emphasizing smooth control movements.
Fixation:
Remind students to maintain a dynamic scan of instruments and external references to avoid fixation on a single point or task, promoting better situational awareness.
Incorrect timing or sequence:
Help students refine their timing and sequencing by providing clear instructions and demonstrations, followed by guided practice to reinforce correct procedures.
Inadequate scan and awareness:
Coach students to improve their scan technique and expand their awareness of the flight environment, emphasizing the importance of vigilance and proactive monitoring.

Desired outcome(s), including completion standards.

Why is it important for flight instructors to clearly define the desired outcome(s) for each maneuver during flight training?
• Helps students understand the specific goals and objectives of the maneuver.
• Provides a target for students to aim for during practice and evaluation.
• Guides students in visualizing and conceptualizing the desired performance.

Why is it important for flight instructors to clearly define the completion standards for each maneuver during flight training?
• Establishes measurable criteria for assessing student performance.
• Ensures consistency and fairness in evaluating student proficiency.
• Helps students gauge their progress and identify areas for improvement.
• Provides a basis for feedback and debriefing sessions.
• Reinforces safety by setting minimum standards for competency in various flight maneuvers.
