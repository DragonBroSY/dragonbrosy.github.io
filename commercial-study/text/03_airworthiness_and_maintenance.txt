§91.213 Inoperative Equipment Flow
Inoperative Equipment. Is there a Minimum Equipment List (MEL)?
Yes: Follow the MEL. *MEL will specify what to do.
No:
- Is it part of the VFR-day type certification instruments and equipment prescribed in the Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS)? Yes: Do Not Fly. No: continue.
- Is it required on the aircraft's equipment list, or on the Kinds of Operations Equipment List (KOEL) for the kind of flight operation being conducted? Yes: Do Not Fly. No: continue.
- Is it required by §91.205 for your type of operation? Yes: Do Not Fly. No: continue.
- Is it required to be operational by an airworthiness directive? Yes: Do Not Fly. No: continue.
What to do if not required:
- Removed from the aircraft, the cockpit control placarded, and the maintenance recorded in accordance with §43.9 of this chapter; (No: Do Not Fly.)
- Or deactivated and placarded "Inoperative." If deactivation of the inoperative instrument or equipment involves maintenance, it must be accomplished and recorded in accordance with part 43 of this chapter; (No: Do Not Fly.)
- And pilot or a person who is rated to perform maintenance (mechanic) on the aircraft, decided that the inoperative instrument or equipment does not constitute a hazard to the aircraft. (No: Do Not Fly.)
Done: Go Fly!
However: Notwithstanding any other provision of this section, an aircraft with inoperable instruments or equipment may be operated under a special flight permit issued in accordance with §21.197 and §21.199.
